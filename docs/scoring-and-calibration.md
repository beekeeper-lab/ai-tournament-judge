# Scoring and Calibration

Before official judging, all judge personas should independently score at least two sample submissions representing a clear weak case and a solid expected case. Use the official evidence format and compare scores, cited evidence, and interpretation of 0–5 anchors.

Calibration may clarify rubric language before the event freezes. It may not force artificial agreement or teach judges a preferred winner. Record changes by incrementing rubric versions.

Monitor per-criterion score distributions, judge means, disagreement rates, `NE` frequency, and adjudication outcomes. Large persistent differences may indicate ambiguous anchors, uneven evidence, or persona overreach. Do not retroactively normalize scores without a published event rule and human approval.

Use deterministic scripts for all arithmetic. Keep raw scores and exact calculations even when reports display rounded values.
